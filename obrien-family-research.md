# O'Brien family research notes

Research date: 22 August 2026.

## Question 1 — Is the IRA volunteer Edward O'Brien related to the Glencolumbkille O'Bryens?

**No connection found.** The memorial image ("Óglach Edward O'Brian, Wexford Brigade, IRA — died on active service in London") is Edward O'Brien of Gorey, Co. Wexford, born 18 September 1974, son of Miley and Margo O'Brien, with a sister Lorraine and brother Gary. He was a Provisional IRA volunteer who died on 18 February 1996 when the 2 kg Semtex device he was carrying detonated prematurely on a number 171 bus in Aldwych, London (the Aldwych bus bombing).

There is no documented genealogical link between this Wexford family and the O'Bryens of Glencolumbkille, parish of Carran, Co. Clare:

- Different county and different line; no source connects the two families.
- Turlough Butler O'Bryen himself left no descendants — the 1911 census shows him and his wife Mary married 11 years with no children.
- O'Brien is one of the most common surnames in Ireland. The image surfaced in a Google image search for "turlough edward o'brien benson" — a name-match artefact of the search engine, not evidence of kinship.

## Question 2 — When was Turlough Butler O'Bryen born?

**1853** (died 1928). Supporting evidence:

- The biography by James K. Watson is titled *The Bee Man of County Clare: Turlough Butler O'Bryen, 1853–1928*.
- The 1911 census records him aged 57, living at Trafalgar Terrace, Dublin, "Instructor in Agriculture", with wife Mary and sister Elizabeth — consistent with a birth in 1853/54.
- A plaque unveiled in May 2019 by Ardee beekeepers states he was **born in Ardee, Co. Louth, in 1853** — notable, since the family estate was at Glencolumbkille, Co. Clare. (The 1870 Clare Journal clipping about his brother James Joseph also mentions an Ardee connection.)
- An exact day/month is not given in accessible sources. Civil birth registration in Ireland only began in 1864, so his birth would be recorded in a parish baptism register (Ardee parish, Co. Louth, if the plaque is right, rather than Carran, Co. Clare).

## Question 3 — Verification of the forum's claims

| Claim (Clare Past Forum thread) | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Terence O'Bryen of Glencolumbkille had seven sons, one (Murtagh) an RIC inspector | Plausible; supported by the marriage records the forum cites (civilrecords.irishgenealogy.ie) but not independently re-verified here |
| James Joseph O'Bryen, army officer, son of the late Terence O'Bryen of Glencollumkille, promoted 1870, died 1920 | Consistent with the Clare Journal (24 Mar 1870) clipping quoted in the thread |
| 1911 census: Turlough Butler O'Bryen, 57, Trafalgar Terrace, Dublin, married 11 years, no children | Matches the census as quoted |
| Turlough claimed the **Earldom of Thomond** and "The O'Brien", lost in the House of Lords to the O'Briens of Dromoland | **Partly garbled.** The Earldom/Marquessate of Thomond became extinct on 3 July 1855 with the death of James O'Brien, 3rd Marquess. The House of Lords case that went "in favour of Dromoland" was the confirmation of Sir Lucius O'Brien of Dromoland as 13th Baron Inchiquin (Committee for Privileges, 11 April 1862). Turlough was only ~9 in 1862, so any Glencolumbkille counter-claim in that proceeding would have been by an older generation; his own assertion of "The O'Brien" chiefship was likely a later, informal claim. Today "The O'Brien, Prince of Thomond" is held by the Baron Inchiquin (Dromoland line). |
| Glencolumbkille O'Bryens were Catholic gentry of Carran parish, Burren, Co. Clare | Confirmed by University of Galway Landed Estates database (O'Brien, Glencolumbkille); in March 1884 Turlough Butler O'Bryen's 396-acre estate at Glencolumbkille South was advertised for sale |
| Bee Man career: appointed 1893 by the Congested Districts Board, travelled by bicycle, pioneered moveable-frame hives, died 1928 (at Inchiquin) | Confirmed by multiple beekeeping-history sources |
